What matters immediately

  • Mixed modality: numeric, categorical, date, and text all contribute signal.
  • Heavy repetition: products recur so often that naive random splits would be misleading.
  • Missingness is informative: process fields are not missing at random.
  • Shortcut risk is real: review-star language and delivery metadata can dominate offline scores.

Executive findings

The strongest predictors are useful, but some of them are dangerously close to workflow artifacts.

What we learned

  • Review stars are explosive: `1/5 stars` pushes fake rate to 89.1%.
  • Missing delivery data is risky: overall fake rate jumps to 63.5% when delivery category is absent.
  • Pricing is not just “cheap = fake”: aggressive overpricing relative to confirmed references is also suspicious.
  • Products repeat constantly: the same names appear over and over, which can inflate apparent generalization.

Business implication

A high-scoring model can still be brittle if it mainly learns workflow shortcuts. The job is not just to predict fake listings; it is to predict them for the right reasons and in a way that survives new products, new merchants, and new review patterns.

Where the trap is

The single most important pattern in this dataset is not a standalone column. It is the interaction between fulfillment mode and missing delivery metadata.

80.0% fake rate across 155 rows. This is far beyond the base rate and likely reflects a workflow state, not just a product signal.

CENTRAL + missing

73.1% fake rate across 160 rows. The same pattern repeats in another operational channel, strengthening the shortcut hypothesis.

MARKETPLACE + missing

32.1% fake rate across 131 rows. Still risky, but notably less extreme than BUSINESS or CENTRAL.

Evaluation reality

This dataset can flatter a model very easily. The evaluation design matters as much as the algorithm choice.

Why random rows are dangerous

  • 11,177 rows belong to repeated product names.
  • The same product families appear across both classes.
  • Entity repetition means a random split can quietly teach the model product identity instead of counterfeit behavior.

Recommended evaluation policy

AreaRecommendation
Primary splitUse a temporal holdout on `PurchDate` instead of naive random rows.
Robustness checkRun a grouped split by `Brand + ProductName` to quantify entity leakage.
CleaningRemove exact duplicates and quarantine conflicting duplicate-like rows.
Primary metricUse PR-AUC and operating-threshold precision/recall, not accuracy alone.

Core takeaway

The model that wins this case study is not the one with the flashiest architecture. It is the one that keeps evaluation honest and avoids overclaiming on shortcut-heavy offline signals.

MVP model choice

TabPFN v2 is the fastest credible baseline for this phase.

Why TabPFN v2 fits this case

  • No long training cycle: it gives us a strong foundation-model baseline immediately.
  • Great for a take-home: small code surface, clear setup, and credible technical story.
  • Works with scalar features: ideal once we convert text into lightweight review and length signals.
  • Good bridge strategy: we can keep the PyTorch hybrid as the next upgrade only if TabPFN leaves headroom.

Decision

Use TabPFN v2 as the first serious model, but keep the governance lens on. This dataset contains enough shortcut signal that the evaluation policy is part of the model design.

1. Run the MVP

Score the cleaned temporal split with TabPFN v2 and inspect validation/test outputs.

2. Stress-test shortcuts

Re-run without review stars and without delivery metadata to measure reliance on brittle signals.

3. Upgrade only if needed

If TabPFN leaves room, move to the custom PyTorch hybrid already scaffolded in the repo.
